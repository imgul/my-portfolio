<TLDR>

Per-student pricing moves with enrolment. A flat price per branch is easier to budget, while a branch price based on size bands offers a middle ground. Choose the model after testing it against small and large schools, onboarding costs and the work included in support.

</TLDR>

A school with 100 students and one with 700 may both have one office, but the same branch price lands very differently on each budget. If you charge per student instead, the invoice can change when admissions or withdrawals change. Both choices need a clear explanation before anyone signs.

This is a guide to comparing models, not a disclosure of Oxygen LMS prices or a record of customer negotiations. The worked prices below are hypothetical, chosen to make the maths visible.

## Three ways to price the same service

| Model | How the invoice works | What to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Per active student | Agreed rate multiplied by a defined student count | Counting rules, minimum charge and changing enrolment |
| Flat per branch | One recurring price for each branch | A small branch and a large one pay the same amount |
| Branch size bands | A flat price for each branch based on its student-count band | Price jumps at band boundaries |

Take a fictional rate of PKR 20 per active student per month and a fictional flat branch rate of PKR 6,000 per month. These are examples, not recommended rates or quotes.

| Active students in one branch | Per student at PKR 20 | Flat branch price | Difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 | PKR 2,000 | PKR 6,000 | Per student is PKR 4,000 less |
| 300 | PKR 6,000 | PKR 6,000 | Equal |
| 700 | PKR 14,000 | PKR 6,000 | Flat is PKR 8,000 less |

The break-even point in this example is 300 students: divide the PKR 6,000 branch price by PKR 20 per student. This comparison assumes the same features, support and payment terms. It says nothing about which model is profitable.

A minimum changes the calculation. If the hypothetical per-student plan has a PKR 3,000 monthly minimum, the 100-student branch pays PKR 3,000 rather than PKR 2,000. Put the minimum next to the rate in a proposal, not in the small print.

## What published prices show

The models already coexist in Pakistan. On its [pricing page](https://www.edusuite.pk/pricing-and-plans/), EduSuite lists Standard at PKR 30 per student per month and Pro at PKR 50. Check the page for the applicable plan terms and inclusions before comparing a quote. <Cite id="edusuite" />

[Edflo’s pricing page](https://www.edflo.io/pricing) lists PKR 2,999 per month for a branch with 1–150 students and PKR 6,999 for 301–700. Each branch is priced on its own size band, with a single owner dashboard. Its monthly figures are billed in advance on the stated billing cycle, not necessarily paid month by month. <Cite id="edflo" />

[Schooliee](https://www.schooliee.com/) describes per-active-student pricing with a 200-student minimum and says setup, import and training are included. A minimum is particularly relevant to a smaller school. <Cite id="schooliee" />

These are vendor-published examples checked on 25 November 2026, not an endorsement or a like-for-like ranking. Prices and terms can change. Ask each vendor for a current written quote covering the same student count, billing period and services.

## Define the count before sending the invoice

“Per student” sounds simple until a child leaves halfway through a month. A quote should say who counts and when. For example, a contract could count enrolled students on the first day of each month, exclude archived records, and apply admissions or withdrawals at the next count. That is one possible rule, not an industry standard.

Also agree how to handle transfers between branches, duplicate records, trial accounts and school holidays. If there is a minimum, state whether it applies per branch or across the organisation. Give the school a count it can check against its own register before the invoice is due.

For bands, explain what happens at the boundary. If a branch grows from 150 to 151 students, does the higher price start immediately, next month or at renewal? A short grace period or a scheduled review can make the increase easier to plan, but either should be written into the offer.

## Price the work around the software

A fee ledger can be cheap to host and still expensive to deliver. Importing a messy register, checking fee heads, training a clerk and answering calls all take time. Track recurring delivery costs separately from one-off setup work so a low subscription does not hide an expensive launch.

For a fictional branch paying PKR 6,000 a month, suppose hosting and routine support cost PKR 1,500 a month. That leaves PKR 4,500 before acquisition, development and other overheads. If setup costs PKR 9,000 and has no separate fee, recovering that cost takes two months at that contribution, assuming the recurring revenue and costs stay unchanged. This is a simplified calculation, not a profit forecast. Stripe’s explanation of SaaS gross margin is a useful starting point for separating direct delivery costs from other expenses. <Cite id="stripe" />

Messaging needs its own line. For example, Twilio describes a WhatsApp platform fee per message and Meta template fees that vary by message type and destination. Estimate volume, check the current rate card and specify an allowance or overage rule instead of promising unlimited messages without knowing the cost. <Cite id="twilio" />

## Make payment terms and discounts visible

Compare monthly and annual offers using the full amount paid. A hypothetical PKR 6,000 monthly subscription costs PKR 72,000 over twelve months. A 10% discount on that total makes an annual advance payment PKR 64,800, or PKR 5,400 per month averaged over the year. The school gives up cash upfront; the vendor gets earlier payment but accepts less revenue. Confirm cancellation, refunds, taxes and renewal prices before treating the two offers as equivalent.

Give discounts a reason and an end date. A temporary onboarding promotion, a multi-branch discount or an annual prepayment discount can each be described on the quote. Record the normal price, the discounted price, the duration and what happens at renewal. Avoid promising custom work or unlimited support as an informal discount: both can continue costing time long after the first invoice.

## A quote a school can check

Before asking for a signature, send a one-page offer with these details:

1. The branch names, student count or band, rate and any minimum.
2. The billing cycle, amount due, taxes, due date and renewal price.
3. What setup, data import and staff training include, and whether they are charged separately.
4. Support hours, messaging allowance, overage charges and any paid integrations.
5. How counts change, what happens at a band boundary, and the cancellation and data export terms.

Run the same quote through a small branch, a large branch and a school with several locations. Then ask whether the amount still covers the time needed to set up and support each one. The number on the price page is only useful if the service behind it can be delivered.

<Takeaways>

- Compare models at several school sizes, using the same features and terms.
- Define active students, minimum charges and band changes in writing.
- Account for import, training, support and messaging before setting a rate.
- Show the full annual payment and discount, plus cancellation and renewal terms.
- Ask for current vendor quotes rather than treating published prices as permanent.

</Takeaways>
